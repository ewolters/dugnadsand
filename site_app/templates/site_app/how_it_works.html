How it works

Technical description of what the system records, what it does not record, and how each commitment can be independently verified.

Four things people share

Hours

Work performed is recorded as a duration against a posting. Thirteen hours on a community garden constitutes a complete entry.

Offers and needs

A single board carries both. Any member may post either, and any member may take up either. Prior contribution is not required and is not consulted.

Projects

A project groups related postings. No owner is assigned. Any member may close a project, including one who never worked on it; restricting closure to an owner would strand a project when that person left.

Material

Documented separately below and in full at the virtual warehouse.

What the record is not

It is not a currency.

Hours cannot be spent, are owed by no one to anyone, and purchase nothing. No code path consults a member's contributions when determining what they may receive.

A member who has contributed nothing may receive without restriction.

Individual contributions appear in the chapter's log under the contributor's name, readable by every member of every organization in it. This is intentional and serves as recognition. No total is computed or displayed at any level: not per member, not per project. That absence is checked by no-aggregate-display, which scans the application source and templates for aggregation over contributions and reports a breach on any match.

Withdrawal is unrestricted. Taking on a posting and then stepping away deletes the claim record entirely; it is not flagged as withdrawn and is not counted. A stored record of withdrawal constitutes a record of non-completion, and any stored value can subsequently be aggregated.

The virtual warehouse

Material, rather than labour, is the more common constraint on a project.

A farm or business registers a location and its contents. Material does not enter the platform's custody at any point. It remains where its holder keeps it; the system stores a location, a description and a quantity. This makes the system a directory rather than a warehouse, with corresponding consequences for title, liability and insurance.

The system evidences that material moved. It does not state a value.

Every quantity is displayed with the age of its last confirmation — confirmed today, not confirmed in four weeks. Only the holder can reset that timestamp. Dispatching material deliberately does not reset it: the sender knows the quantity sent but has not recounted the remainder. Unconfirmed listings are de-emphasised rather than hidden, since the material may still be present.

Dispatched material produces a manifest carrying a QR code. The recipient scans it to confirm receipt. No account or registration is required, since requiring one would mean the receipt is not recorded at all.

Bills of material

A project may specify required material as well as labour.

A line specifies a description, a quantity and a unit. Arrivals are recorded against it, and material may be dispatched directly from a warehouse listing so that the manifest and the project record the same movement.

Hours and material are held in separate records and are never combined. No field records the value of material, and no field expresses material as an equivalent number of hours. Either would assign a price to a donation.

One aggregate is displayed: the quantity outstanding on a line. It is a property of the requirement rather than of any member, and material cannot be coordinated without it.

Chapters, and what a chapter shares

An organization is a party in the network — a household, a one-person business, a not-for-profit. A chapter is the organizations covering one area, and it is the boundary that matters: members of every organization in a chapter see one another's offers, needs, projects and material, and may take up any of it.

Between chapters there is none of that, and an organization in no chapter is visible only to itself. Separation is enforced by row-level security in the database rather than by application-level filtering, and fails closed: a defect causes rows to disappear rather than to leak. Authorship is unaffected — every record carries the organization of whoever wrote it.

Data crossing the system boundary — notifications, email, integrations — carries only the existence of a record and a link to it. Message text and member names are excluded. Both are retrieved inside the organization when the recipient signs in.

How to check any of this

Each statement above is a claim, and is bound to an executable check.

Results are published at /attestation/ and are executed when that page is requested, so that a stalled scheduler cannot present a stored pass as a current one.

ClaimWhat it means
no-gatingNo code path consults what a member has given when deciding what they may receive
no-balanceNo record carries a balance, a credit limit, or a spendable total
no-material-valuationNo record of material carries a value, a price, or an equivalence in hours
no-obligationOffers are ceilings. Nothing records a minimum, a completion, or a penalty for stopping
no-aggregate-displayNo per-member total is computed or displayed

A check whose subject does not yet exist reports incomplete rather than passing, and a run cannot report an overall pass while any check is in that state.

Not a legal attestation This states what the code was checked for and what the checks returned. It is not legal advice, it is not a tax opinion, and it does not determine an organization's obligations; those are matters for that organization's own counsel and accountant. It describes the behaviour of the software. That behaviour is testable.

There is also a formal statement of operating policy — the same commitments set out for a board or an adviser, including a section on what is deliberately not promised.

The code is public at github.com/ewolters/dugnadsand. The claims live in policy/manifest.toml, the checks behind them in policy/checks.py, and the reasoning in docs/design-rules.md.
