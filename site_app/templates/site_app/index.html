Dugnad · Norwegian

“The work we do together.”

Nobody sends an invoice.

Dugnad is a Norwegian word. It's the Saturday the whole street turns out to clear the lot or paint the community hall, and nobody keeps track of who owed what. There's usually coffee and something to eat when it's done.

The entire ledger

We write down what happened. Never what it was worth.

Thirteen hours on a community garden. Two hundred board-feet of oak toward a roof. Both are recorded; neither is priced. The two records are kept separate and are never combined: an hour and a plank are different units, and equating them would assign a price to both. Neither record can be spent or exhausted.

Nothing is bought or sold.

There is no currency and no marketplace. A surplus is posted and whoever needs it takes it. No accumulated hours are required in order to receive, and giving returns nothing.

Nothing is owed.

Recorded hours are not a claim on anyone else's time. No member holds a debt or a deficit, and an empty record carries no consequence.

Material counts as well as time.

Farms and businesses can list material and its location. It remains their property until someone needs it, it is never assigned a value, and receipt is confirmed by the recipient at the point of collection.

Stopping is always permitted.

An offer of four hours is a ceiling of four, not a floor. Withdrawing partway through records nothing and carries no penalty.

Getting started in upstate South Carolina.

The project is early: a small number of participants, a substantial list of open questions, and a decision not to build anything that keeps score.

Organizations in the region are welcome to make contact. The mechanics are documented in how it works and the commitments in the statement of operating policy.
