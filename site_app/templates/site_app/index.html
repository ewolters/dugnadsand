Dugnad · Norwegian

Nobody sends an invoice.

Dugnad is a Norwegian word. It's the Saturday the whole street turns out to clear the lot or paint the community hall, and nobody keeps track of who owed what. There's usually coffee and something to eat when it's done.

The entire ledger

We keep one record: hours.

Thirteen hours on a community garden. That's the whole ledger — we write down what you did, and that's where it ends. The hours don't buy anything, and nobody owes them to you. You can't spend them and you can't run out of them.

Nothing is bought or sold.

There's no currency here and no marketplace. If you grew more potatoes than you can eat, put them up and whoever needs them takes them. You don't need hours saved to take something, and you don't get anything back for giving.

Nothing is owed.

Your hours aren't a claim on anybody else's time. Nobody's in debt, nobody's behind, and turning up with an empty record costs you nothing at all.

You can stop whenever.

Offer four hours and you're offering up to four, never at least four. Change your mind halfway through and nothing happens to you. We built it that way on purpose.

Getting started in upstate South Carolina.

We're early. Right now it's a few neighbors, a long list of questions we haven't answered yet, and a decision not to build anything that keeps score.

If this sounds like your street, tell us about it.
