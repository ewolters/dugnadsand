Terms of participation

Version {{ terms_version }} · agreed by every organization on admission

An organization admitted to Dugnadsand agrees to these terms. They set out what the network does, what it does not do, and who is answerable for what. They are written to be read once and understood, rather than to be long.

Three documents, three jobs

The statement of operating policy describes what the software does and binds each commitment to a check that runs against the source. The acceptable use policy is the standard of conduct applied by people. This page is the agreement. Where the three touch the same subject, this page governs the relationship between an organization and the network.

1. What the network is

Dugnadsand is a place where organizations post what they have and what they need, talk to each other, and keep their own records. It is operated by the entity named at the foot of this page.

The network does not perform work, supervise work, direct anybody, employ anybody, or stand between two organizations that have decided to help each other. It carries messages and records. Everything else happens between people, off this system.

Admission is registration, not endorsement

An organization tells the network what it holds — a licence, a certificate of insurance, a tax number — and that statement is recorded as made. The network does not confirm it and does not vouch for it. Admission is not a representation by the network that any organization is competent, insured, licensed, solvent, or safe to work with. An officer may look at a document before deciding and may record that they did; that is one person's note on one day and it is published to nobody.

2. What an organization is responsible for

An admitted organization is responsible, entirely and without reference to the network, for:

  • Its own licences, registrations and permits, and for holding whatever the work it offers actually requires.
  • Its own insurance, in whatever kinds and amounts its work requires.
  • The safety of the work it does and of the people who do it, including anybody it brings with it.
  • Its own compliance with law — employment, tax, safety, licensing, and anything else that applies to it.
  • Everything it says on this system, and everything it does after saying it.
  • Its own decisions about whom to help, whom to work alongside, and what to accept.

Nothing on this system reduces any of that, and nothing on this system should be relied on as having checked any of it.

Offers of licensed work

Where an organization has told the network it holds a licence to practise, it affirms each time it posts an offer, or puts its name to somebody else's, that the offer stands under that licence, that the licence is current, and that the work is within what the licence covers. That affirmation is the organization's own statement. The network records that it was made and which licence was named, and confirms nothing.

3. People who ask for help

Somebody who asks for help through this site is not a member of the network and is not a party to these terms. Their request is shown to the mutual aid groups covering their area and to nobody else, and their name and contact details are withheld until one group takes it up.

What happens after that is between that group and that person. The network is not a party to it, records no outcome, and makes no promise to anybody that help will arrive, that it will arrive in time, or that it will be any good. A group that takes up a request takes on whatever duty arises from doing so, and takes it on alone.

4. Indemnity

An admitted organization will indemnify the operator against claims, losses and reasonable costs arising out of that organization's own acts and omissions — the work it performs, the goods it transfers, what it posts here, its dealings with anybody who asked for help, and any failure to hold the licences or insurance its work requires.

This does not extend to anything caused by the operator's own negligence or wilful misconduct, and it is not a transfer of liability the organization would not otherwise have. It says that where an organization causes harm, the organization answers for it and the network does not answer for it in its place.

5. Limits

The network is provided as it is. The operator makes no warranty that it will be available, that anything posted on it is accurate, or that any organization on it is what it says it is. Every disclaimer here is stated plainly rather than in capitals, and is meant exactly as written.

The operator is not liable for indirect or consequential loss, for lost opportunity, or for anything arising from work done or not done between organizations or between an organization and a person who asked for help. Where liability cannot lawfully be excluded, it is limited to the amount paid to the operator for use of the network, which is nothing — participation is free and always has been.

Nothing here excludes liability for death or personal injury caused by the operator's negligence, for fraud, or for anything else the law does not permit to be excluded.

6. No agency

Admission creates no partnership, joint venture, employment, agency or franchise between an organization and the operator, or between two organizations. No organization may hold itself out as speaking for the network, and the network does not speak for any of them.

7. Data

What the network holds and for how long is described in the statement of operating policy. Two things belong here because they are commitments rather than descriptions:

  • The name and contact details of somebody who asked for help are erased when the request is closed, and in any case within ninety days of it being made.
  • An organization's records stay scoped to its own chapter and are never sold, licensed or given to anybody for marketing.

8. Ending it

An organization may leave at any time. A chapter officer may remove an organization from a chapter, with a reason that is recorded. Removal does not delete what the organization wrote and does not disable anybody's login: the organization simply stops sharing with that chapter, as one admitted into no chapter always has.

9. Changes

These terms are versioned. Changing them does not silently re-characterise what an organization already agreed to — the version agreed is recorded against each admission, and a material change asks for agreement again.
