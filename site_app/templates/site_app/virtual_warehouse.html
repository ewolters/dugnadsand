The virtual warehouse

Willing hands are rarely what a project runs short of. A pallet of shingles is. Somebody nearby usually has them already — this is how they find each other, and what it deliberately refuses to do along the way.

Nothing moves until somebody needs it

The goods never enter our custody. We record a location, a description and a count — and we can prove a thing moved, without ever saying what it was worth.

That is not modesty. No custody means no title, and no title means no storage liability, no insurance obligation, and nothing that turns a directory into a warehouse. It is the same shape that keeps the record of hours a record rather than a currency.

Every amount says when somebody last looked

A quantity with no date on it is a claim about the present tense that nobody checked, and somebody drives forty miles on it. So every listing carries the age of its confirmation — confirmed today, not confirmed in four weeks — and two rules keep that honest.

Only the holder can move that clock. Anybody else asserting what is in somebody's barn is guessing. And shipping something out deliberately does not: the sender knows what they sent, but they have not re-counted what is left, and letting a shipment reset the clock would make a shelf look freshly checked because something left it.

Listings that have gone quiet are dimmed, never hidden. The material may well still be there — only its holder can say.

What the paperwork looks like

Material moving out produces a manifest. It travels with the goods, and whoever takes delivery scans the code to sign for it — no account, no sign-up, because they are standing in a yard with a phone and a receipt nobody can record is a receipt that never happens.

This records what moved and when. It states no value, and no value will be added to it.

What it will not do

It will not price anything.
What material is worth is a question for the donor, their adviser and the tax authority. Any figure this system produced would be an appraisal of donated property written by a platform about a donor, which is the one document it must never generate. A manifest is evidence that goods moved; a valuation is a different document, and only one of the two is safe for us to write.
It will not convert material into hours.
“Two hundred board-feet became forty hours” reads like bookkeeping and is an exchange rate. Hours and material are two separate records, side by side, never added together.
It will not sort your material into types.
There are no categories and no icons for kinds of thing, because a fixed vocabulary of materials makes two donations comparable — and comparable donations have a price. Descriptions are whatever you would say out loud. Where the system needs to match a need to something on a shelf, it matches on the unit you typed: board-feet to board-feet.
It will not tell you the shelf is full when nobody has looked.
Absence of a recent confirmation is shown as exactly that, rather than quietly passed off as availability.

For a business thinking about listing something

You keep the goods, the space and the title. You decide what goes on the list, you are the only person who can confirm what is still there, and you are told when something is booked out of a place you hold — told, not asked, because material on a list is already on offer and a permission step would put a gate in front of a gift.

What you get back is evidence of transfer: a record of what left, when, where it went and that somebody signed for it. What you will not get from us is a number for your tax return, and that is deliberate.

The commitments behind all of this are set out in the statement of operating policy and checked continuously at the attestation. How it works covers the rest of the system.
